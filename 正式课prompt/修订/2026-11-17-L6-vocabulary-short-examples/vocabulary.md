# L6 first encounter with a vocabulary word

Use the current target and intended meaning in renderContent. Teach it in the opening through two short phrases or mini-examples, then an easy, optional meaning check. Use the actual picture only where the description supports it. The second example can be an ordinary or clearly imagined situation. These examples are for listening and understanding. Do not ask the learner to say them back.

## Opening turn
1. Name the target and give its plain meaning in a few familiar words. Use the same form or sense the card teaches.
2. Give exactly two distinct short examples, ideally 2 to 6 words each. Use a natural collocation, phrase or mini-sentence. Choose from the bank below. Say each as its own spoken unit separated by a period. No definitions inside the examples, added reasons, long sentences or extra exposition. Total opening before the question should usually be under 25 words.
3. Ask one very short meaning check about the picture, examples or the simple meaning you just taught. The child should be able to answer with one or two words. A quick check of a meaning just shown is intentional here and overrides the shared rule requiring new information in a question. Do not ask why, ask for a full sentence, ask the child to invent an example, or test new animal facts. Use two familiar concrete choices when possible. Only one question. Use [STUDENT_TALK]. If there is no useful, easy check, finish after the examples with [TEMPLATE_FINISH].

## Example bank
Use only the current target's row. The wording below is short spoken material, not a set of stages or labels to read aloud.
- adaptation. A feature that helps an animal live. A giraffe's long neck. A polar bear's thick fur. Easy check: Which helps a giraffe reach high leaves, its neck or its tail?
- structural. About how a body is built. A bird's wings. A fish's fins. Easy check: A body part or an action?
- adapt to. Become better suited to a place or condition. Adapt to the cold. Adapt to dry weather. Easy check: Which is colder, a desert or a snowy place? Tie the question to the phrase instead when possible, e.g. Which phrase fits a snowy place, adapt to the cold or adapt to the heat? Use one short version. For animal body changes, never imply an individual decides to change.
- survive. Stay alive. Survive the winter. Survive without food. Easy check: Does survive mean stay alive or fall asleep?
- behavior. What an animal does. Hiding from danger. Sleeping during the day. Easy check: Which is a behavior, hiding or having long ears?
- characteristic. A feature that helps describe something. A tiger's stripes. A giraffe's long neck. Easy check: Which describes a tiger, stripes or spots?
- school. A group of fish. A school of small fish. A school swimming together. Easy check: One fish or many fish together?
- hunt. Look for animals to eat. A lion hunts. Hunt for food. Easy check: Looking for food or going to sleep?
- protect. Keep safe. Protect the cubs. A helmet protects your head. Easy check: What does a helmet protect, your head or your foot?
- instinct. Something an animal does without being taught. A hatchling heads to sea. A newborn cries. Easy check: Born ready or taught later? Do not claim birds can fly without practice.

## After the learner responds
Reply to the exact idea, choice or question in one brief sentence. A short answer counts. If they are confused, give one simple clarification or a different short phrase. If they are quiet or want to move on, offer a brief meaning takeaway without praise. End [TEMPLATE_FINISH]. This card has one invitation at most. Never ask a second question, ask for repetition, or recycle the first two examples as a recap. English only. Plain spoken text. No action tags.
